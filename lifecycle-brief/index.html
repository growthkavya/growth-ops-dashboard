The belief that turned out to be wrong

We have always said that once a student does CFA Level 1 with us, they almost always come back for Level 2 and Level 3. It is in our strategy documents. It shapes how we think about lifetime value.

We measured it across all 85,802 students in our records. It is not true.

On cohorts older than three years, CFA L1 to L2 rises to 25.9%. Still nowhere near "almost always". These are purchases visible in our own records, so the true number may be a little higher where students bought elsewhere. That does not change the conclusion.

Why this matters more than any other number here. We spend heavily to acquire a Level 1 student. If 87 of every 100 never buy the next level from us, our real lifetime value is a fraction of what we assume, and the cheapest revenue available to us is being left on the table. Not because students are unhappy, but because after they pay, we mostly stop talking to them.

The money we are already leaving behind

Students are buying extensions with nobody selling them

2,226 students whose access ran out bought a validity extension entirely on their own initiative. No campaign, no call, no reminder. That is proof that post purchase selling works here and that it is completely unmanaged.

We do not know when most students sit their exam

Attempt is recorded for only 38.5% of students. This single missing field blocks 7 of the 12 things we plan to do after purchase, because almost every post purchase conversation is timed off the exam date.

We never find out who passed

There is no result field anywhere in our systems. Results do not come to us automatically and never will. So we have to ask, on result day, every cycle. That one question unlocks the entire next level conversation.

The gap between purchases is long, and we go quiet inside it

The median gap between a student's first and last purchase is 515 days. Well over a year in which almost nothing structured reaches them from us. That gap is where the ladder leak happens.

Two simple ideas run the whole thing

Everyone in the system, lead or student, is scored on exactly two axes. Before payment they answer one pair of questions, after payment another. Learn these two ideas and you understand the entire plan.

Before payment

Fit and Intent

Fit asks whether this course genuinely suits them: their stage, eligibility, time and budget. Intent asks how much they want it right now: what they have opened, clicked, attended and asked for.

High fit and low intent means keep nurturing. Low fit and high intent means talk to them, but about a different course. High on both means call today.
After payment

Headroom and Momentum

Headroom asks how much more they could reasonably buy: where they sit on the ladder, what they have spent, what related courses they do not own. Momentum asks whether they are still active: watch time, pace, mocks, replies.

Headroom decides what we offer them. Momentum decides whether we need to step in first. Momentum decays when they go quiet, so we notice before they disappear.

The important part: Fit and Headroom are structural, they change slowly. Intent and Momentum are behavioural, they move every week and they fade with silence. That is why nobody in the system stays hot forever and nobody gets forgotten forever either.

Counsellor queues are sorted by this, so the top of your list is genuinely the most valuable call you can make that morning. Every stage also has a stuck time. A lead sitting in Ready to buy for 72 hours with no counsellor action surfaces to the manager automatically, because that is the most expensive place to lose someone.

40 guard rules sit above all of it. These are the things the system must never do, whatever the score says: no acquisition messaging to a paying student, no payment link before eligibility is confirmed, no course named to someone whose course we only guessed, nothing at all to anyone who opted out. Each rule has a severity and a plain explanation, so when a message is blocked, anyone can see why.

After payment: the half we have never built

This is the genuinely new part. Once a student pays, they move through nine stages driven by their exam calendar rather than their clicks, because after purchase the dates matter more than the behaviour.

Just paid Getting started Studyingthe long middle Exam run-up60 days out Result awaited Passed Did not pass Skipped the exam Inactive

And there are exactly six moments where a student would genuinely want to buy something more. Everything after enrolment is built around these.

1
First enrolment

The one moment we currently manage well.

2
Validity extension

Access runs out before they are ready. Already earning Rs 1 crore unprompted.

3
Next attempt

They did not pass, or skipped the exam. Support first, sell second.

4
Next level on the ladder

They passed. This is the 13% problem, and the biggest prize.

5
Adjacent product

They finished, or want job skills alongside the certification.

6
Referral

Possible any time, strongest in the days right after a pass.

Result day is the hinge. Results never reach us on their own, so on every result day we ask every student in that cycle a single question: how did it go. That one answer routes them into congratulations and the next level, or into support and a re-attempt, or tells us they did not sit the exam at all. Without it, moments 3, 4 and 6 cannot happen.

What this means in practice

  • One queue, not two. Leads and students sit in the same priority list. A student two weeks from their exam can rank above a fresh lead, because dates beat money in the ordering.
  • Nobody is permanently lost. Parked people carry a stated reason and a return date. When the reason expires, they come back into play automatically.
  • Counsellors get a shorter, better list. Sorted by real priority, with the reason attached, so the first call of the day is the most valuable one available.
  • Marketing can finally target properly. Cohorts become real, maintained lists, so a CFA Level 1 student never receives a Level 1 acquisition ad again.
  • One field unlocks most of this. Capturing exam attempt at enrolment, and asking the result question on result day, turns on the majority of the post purchase plan. It is the cheapest high value change available to us.
