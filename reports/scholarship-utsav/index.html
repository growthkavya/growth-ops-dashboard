The one-paragraph story: with the full payment history in view (24 Mar – 19 Aug), both campaigns beat their plans. Together they returned ₹140.98L against ₹27.02L spent — 5.22x versus the 4.6x we projected (113% of projected revenue). The scholarship delivered 5.68x against its 5x target with 508 buyers (130% of the 390 planned); Utsav nearly doubled its plan at 3.97x. We got there on volume, not price — average ticket ran ₹26,401 against the ₹30k assumed, but far more people bought than we planned for. The number that matters most: 253 of the 534 buyers (47%) are brand-new students worth ₹81.04L — 3.00x ROAS on net-new business alone, before counting a single rupee of existing-student upsell. And these figures are counted strictly: ₹42.13L of pre-exam purchases by old or existing leads were denied campaign credit under the agreed attribution rules, and buyers we can't link by email/phone aren't counted at all.

4.2 New vs upsell — who the revenue came from

The majority of scholarship revenue — 56% — came from genuinely new students, worth ₹74.43L; on new students alone the campaign returned 3.18x, more than covering the entire spend. On top of that, existing SSEI students added ₹50.84L of upsell (L2, L3, Analyst Stack — counted as campaign-driven per the agreed rules), and 34 previously-inactive students came back and bought ₹7.73L — the reactivation opportunity the April report flagged, partially cashed in.

4.3 IVR campaigns — did calling correlate with buying?

Paid IVR (platform-familiarity script, 5,530 called)
Unpaid IVR (payment-nudge script, 2,835 called)
Both call campaigns show the same pattern: people who engaged with the call bought more. Among paid students, key-pressers converted at 7.5–9.5% versus 5.3% for those who never picked up. Even on the unpaid list — where overall conversion was a thin 1.8% — the roughly 100 people who pressed a key converted at 7–10%, several times their peers. A fair warning on reading this: correlation, not causation — pressing a key doesn't cause a purchase, it reveals intent. Which is exactly why it's useful: IVR key-press data is a ready-made priority list for counsellors, and it was sitting in a call log.

7. Key insights & learnings

1
Both campaigns beat their plans: 5.22x combined against the 4.6x projected. ₹140.98L came back on ₹27.02L spent — 113% of projected revenue, verified payment by payment. The scholarship landed at 5.68x (target 5x) and Utsav at 3.97x (target 2.1x). Every rupee counted here survived the strictest reading of the attribution rules.
2
A lesson we almost learned the hard way: never judge a campaign on a partial ledger. With only the 25-May-onwards data, this same analysis read 2.85x for the scholarship — a 'miss'. The full history from campaign launch shows 5.68x — more than half of all campaign revenue arrived in the very window that was missing. Day-90 and day-180 attribution reruns on complete data should be standard ritual for every future campaign.
3
Volume beat plan, ticket didn't — and that trade won. 534 buyers against the 415 planned (129%), at ₹26,401 average ticket versus ₹30,000 assumed. Cost per acquired customer came in at ₹4,610 for the scholarship — well under the ₹6,005 the plan implied.
4
The number to run the business on: 253 brand-new students, ₹81.04L, 3.00x on new business alone. Existing students convert ~3.5x more easily (18.9% vs 5.4% among exam takers) — many of them would have bought something anyway, which is why every table in this report now splits new from existing. Even judged purely on new students, both campaigns cleared their spend comfortably; the ₹50.84L of existing-student upsell is a bonus on top, not the story.
5
Look at total revenue and organic wins; look at NEW students and Meta wins — both are true, and the budget should know it. Organic Platforms made the most money (₹65.74L) but 60% of it came from people already in our orbit. Meta brought in 82 brand-new buyers — the most of any channel — with 66% of its ₹39.74L being net-new (1.47x on new business alone; Google similar at 4.19x overall). Translation: paid media is the acquisition engine, our own platforms are the monetisation engine — fund them as different jobs, not competitors.
6
Influencers and auto-branding remain the two lines with nothing to show. ₹3.00L of spend produced 1 traceable sale (₹41,100) between them. Third campaign in a row of weak signal — cut them or restructure to pay-on-performance.
7
The ₹99 exam fee is the best qualifier we have. Exam takers bought at 8.7% — 4.2x the rate of free registrants (2.1%) — and carry 71% of scholarship revenue. Mock-test takers behaved almost identically (8.3%). Small paid commitments and practice-content engagement are the two intent signals worth building every future funnel around.
8
The IVR call logs are an untapped lead-scoring goldmine. Across both campaigns, key-pressers converted at 7–10% versus ~5% for non-pickups on the paid list and ~1% on the unpaid list. That signal was sitting in a call log the whole time — pipe key-press events straight into counsellor priority queues.
9
Utsav no-shows were worth ₹8.29L — 58% of Utsav revenue. Attendees converted at 5.6% (offline attendees at 13.9%), so attendance clearly multiplies value — but the registration list itself is the durable asset. Nurture streams must include no-shows by default.
10
The buying window is ~120 days, anchored to batch starts. April (the campaign month) was the biggest single month, then collections held a steady ₹32–34L monthly plateau through July, riding each CFA L1 batch-start date. Two actions: schedule batch-start offers inside 60 days of any future exam, and rerun this attribution at day-180 (~27 Oct 2026) to capture the tail.

8. Methodology, data sources & caveats

How attribution was computed

  1. The purchase registers: two sources were merged — the payment-gateway export (4,422 paid orders, 24 Mar – 19 Aug 2026; 154 zero-rupee free enrollments and 116 failed payments excluded) and the manual collections ledger (1,272 entries kept after removing 1,248 entries that duplicated gateway orders — same person, same amount, within 3 days). We scanned all 5,694 resulting payments with one purpose — finding campaign buyers. Payments from people with no link to either campaign are not part of this report.
  2. The pre-exam rule (agreed 19 Aug) — now active: an order paid before 28 Apr earns campaign credit only if the buyer became a lead after the 24 Mar scholarship announcement and is a genuinely new lead. This rule did real work: ₹42,12,916 of pre-exam purchases by old or existing leads was denied campaign credit (137 buyers excluded entirely, plus partial exclusions for buyers who also purchased later). The headline numbers above are net of this.
  3. Matching: buyer email OR any phone number (normalised to 10 digits, multi-number cells split) matched against (a) the 9,624-lead scholarship LeadSquared base with channel + SSEI classification — the same classified file behind the April report — and (b) the consolidated Utsav lead list (2,400 unique after removing 14 internal test rows).
  4. Buckets: matched both lists → "Scholarship + Utsav"; one list → that campaign; neither → "Individual" (excluded from campaign ROAS).
  5. Revenue rule: every collected rupee from a matched buyer counts (course fees, part-payments, extensions, books); unique persons = conversions; books/extension-only buyers flagged (143 of 534). Existing-SSEI purchases count as campaign upsell, reported separately.
  6. Accuracy audit (two independent recounts + identity checks): the attributed totals — ₹1,40,98,091 / ₹1,33,00,680 / ₹14,28,311 — were reproduced twice, once person-by-person and once payment-by-payment with separately written code, including an independent implementation of the pre-exam rule: exact match to the rupee, both times. 83% of matched buyers matched on email (same inbox = same person); phone-only matches were name-checked against the lead record in the earlier audit round with a worst-case ambiguity of ~0.2% of attributed revenue. Zero students double-counted. Channel and student-type splits each sum exactly to 508; campaign baselines tie back to the April report (9,624 leads / 6,472 paid / 4,008 exam / 2,869 mock).

Read the numbers with these caveats

1. Still slightly conservative, though far less than before. Online orders are now complete from campaign launch (24 Mar). What may still be missing: offline/manual collections before 25 May (the manual ledger starts there), and any buyer who purchased with a different email and phone than they registered with. Both can only push the real numbers up.
2. This is cash received, not deal value. Where a student pays in installments, only the money that actually arrived in the window is counted — some enrollments are worth more than what shows here.
3. Patterns, not proof. The IVR, attendance and exam-stage comparisons show which groups buy more — they don't prove the activity caused the purchase.
4. Utsav universe: attribution ran on the consolidated 2,400-lead file supplied Aug 2026; April's headline counted 2,549 raw registrations (incl. 618 LP leads with broken UTM). Attendance in this file: 427 vs 448 in April (LP attendee records not all present).
5. Aug 2026 is partial (to 19 Aug) and the pipeline remains open — recommend re-running this exact attribution at 27 Oct 2026 (day-180).
