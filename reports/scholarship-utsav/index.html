Per attribution rules, existing-student purchases post-campaign count as campaign-driven upsell. Net-new alone = 1.19x ROAS; upsell + reactivation lift it to 2.85x. 7. Key insights & learnings

1
The bottom line: 2.77x confirmed, against the 4.6x we projected. ₹74.76L has come back on ₹27.02L spent — 60% of the projected revenue, banked and verified. Add the unrecorded first month, the buyers we can't link by contact details, and a still-open pipeline, and the true figure sits comfortably higher. 2.77x is the number we can defend line by line — the floor, not the ceiling.
2
Utsav beat its plan on every money metric. 2.53x against a 2.1x target, 121% of projected revenue — and the 1% conversion assumption we wrote in April landed at 1.04%. Almost eerily accurate. At ₹3.60L of spend, it was the cheaper and more predictable of the two bets.
3
Organic out-earned paid roughly 2.3-to-1. Our own platforms and referrals brought in ₹44.75L with almost no direct spend, while Meta + Google returned ₹20.96L on ₹19.45L (1.08x). That doesn't make paid ads a failure — they filled the funnel — but it does mean our community and referral channels are the profit engine, and they deserve investment as such.
4
Influencers and auto-branding: ₹3.00L spent, zero traceable sales. The April report already showed thin lead numbers from both (229 + 23 leads); the purchase register confirms not one of them bought. Either cut these lines or move them to pay-on-performance deals.
5
The ₹99 exam fee is doing exactly what it was designed to do — sorting the serious from the curious. Students who sat the exam bought a course at 3.3%; those who registered but never paid the ₹99 bought at just 0.8% — a 4.2x gap. Exam takers alone carry 65% of scholarship revenue. Small paid commitments should be a standard feature of future campaigns.
6
Half the scholarship money came from students we already had. Existing SSEI students bought ₹33.79L worth (51%) — upselling to L2, L3 and Analyst Stack — versus ₹27.97L from genuinely new students. That's a win (it deepens student lifetime value), but it changes the math: on new students alone the scholarship returned 1.19x. Next campaign's targets should model new business and upsell separately.
7
We got the ticket size right and the volume wrong. Average purchase came in at ₹33,981 versus the ₹30,000 we assumed — better than plan. What fell short was the number of buyers: 220 against a combined target of 415. The lesson for the next projection is to pressure-test the conversion-rate assumption, not the pricing one.
8
The IVR calls turned out to be a sorting tool, not a selling tool. Among paid students, those who picked up converted ~35% better than those who didn't — useful signal for prioritising counsellor time. But the payment-nudge calls to unpaid registrants produced almost nothing (0.7%), and the 53 people who pressed for a payment link bought zero courses. Keep the engagement-check calls, feed the key-press data to counsellors, drop the nudge script.
9
Utsav no-shows turned out to be worth real money. 62% of Utsav revenue came from people who registered but never showed up — including the single biggest Utsav sale, a ₹92,100 CFA L1 + Analyst Stack combo. The registration list is the asset; attendance is a bonus. No-shows must stay in every nurture stream.
10
Most of the buying happened in a ~90-day window. Collections peaked in June–July, right on the CFA L1 batch starts (3 Jun, 26 Jun, 17 Jul), and thinned by August. Two takeaways: line up batch-start offers within 60 days of any future exam, and make a day-90 and day-180 rerun of this attribution a standard ritual — the next one is due ~27 Oct 2026.

8. Methodology, data sources & caveats

How attribution was computed

  1. The purchase registers: two sources were merged — the payment-gateway export (10 online orders, paid-status only) and the manual collections ledger (2,520 entries) — covering 25 May – 19 Aug 2026. Duplicate entries appearing in both (same person, amount and day) were removed: 0 found. We scanned all 2,530 resulting payments with one purpose — finding campaign buyers. Payments from people with no link to either campaign are not part of this report. (3 matched rows had a blank amount; those buyers are counted, their revenue as ₹0.)
  2. The pre-exam rule (agreed 19 Aug): an order paid before 28 Apr earns campaign credit only if the buyer became a lead after the 24 Mar scholarship announcement and is a genuinely new lead — old leads buying before the exam get no credit. In the current data the earliest payment is 25 May, so this rule had nothing to exclude; it stands ready if earlier orders are backfilled.
  3. Matching: buyer email OR any phone number (normalised to 10 digits, multi-number cells split) matched against (a) the 9,624-lead scholarship LeadSquared base with channel + SSEI classification — the same classified file behind the April report — and (b) the consolidated Utsav lead list (2,400 unique after removing 14 internal test rows).
  4. Buckets: matched both lists → "Scholarship + Utsav"; one list → that campaign; neither → "Individual" (excluded from campaign ROAS).
  5. Revenue rule: every collected rupee from a matched buyer counts (course fees, part-payments, extensions, books); unique persons = conversions; books/extension-only buyers flagged (9 of 220). Existing-SSEI purchases count as campaign upsell, reported separately.
  6. Accuracy audit (two independent recounts + identity checks): the attributed totals — ₹74,75,911 / ₹66,85,711 / ₹9,08,800 — were reproduced twice, once person-by-person and once payment-by-payment with separately written code: exact match both times. ~81% of matched buyers matched on email (same inbox = same person). The remaining phone-only matches were name-checked against the lead record: 3 flagged differences are the same person with spelling variations; exactly 1 (₹13,500) could be a family member on a shared phone. Worst-case attribution error: 0.2% of attributed revenue. Zero students double-counted. Channel and student-type splits each sum exactly to 202; all campaign baselines tie back to the April report (9,624 leads / 6,472 paid / 4,008 exam / 2,869 mock).

Read the numbers with these caveats

1. These are minimum confirmed numbers. The purchase register begins 25 May, so anyone who bought in the four weeks right after the 26–27 Apr exam — typically the hottest period — is invisible to this analysis. And a student who bought using a different email or phone than they registered with can't be linked. Real attributed revenue is therefore somewhat higher than shown; it cannot be lower.
2. This is cash received, not deal value. Where a student pays in installments, only the money that actually arrived in the window is counted — some enrollments are worth more than what shows here.
3. Patterns, not proof. The IVR, attendance and exam-stage comparisons show which groups buy more — they don't prove the activity caused the purchase.
4. Utsav universe: attribution ran on the consolidated 2,400-lead file supplied Aug 2026; April's headline counted 2,549 raw registrations (incl. 618 LP leads with broken UTM). Attendance in this file: 427 vs 448 in April (LP attendee records not all present).
5. Aug 2026 is partial (to 19 Aug) and the pipeline remains open — recommend re-running this exact attribution at 27 Oct 2026 (day-180).
