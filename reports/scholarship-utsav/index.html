4.1 Funnel depth predicts purchase

Conversion rate to a course purchase by scholarship funnel stage. Exam takers convert at 4.2x the rate of registrants who never paid the ₹99 — the ₹99 exam funnel is a genuine intent qualifier, exactly as designed. Exam takers alone account for 65% of scholarship revenue.

4.2 New vs upsell — who the revenue came from

Per attribution rules, existing-student purchases post-campaign count as campaign-driven upsell. Net-new alone = 1.19x ROAS; upsell + reactivation lift it to 2.85x. The scholarship was as much a re-engagement engine (58% of revenue) as an acquisition engine. The April report flagged the 244 in-system-inactive exam payers as a reactivation opportunity — 14 of that cohort's segment did buy (₹5.10L).

4.3 IVR campaigns — did calling correlate with buying?

Paid IVR (platform-familiarity script, 5,530 called)
Unpaid IVR (payment-nudge script, 2,835 called)
On the paid-student IVR, everyone who engaged (picked up: 2.8%) out-converted the not-picked group (2.1%) — a ~35% relative lift; "pressed 1 / knows the platform" was the single best segment at 3.7%. The unpaid-nudge IVR, as the April report suspected ("low conversion intent"), delivered almost nothing: 19 course buyers from 2,835 calls (0.7%), and the 53 who asked for a payment link produced zero. Correlation, not causation — but the sorting power is real: IVR response is a usable lead-scoring signal.

7. Key insights & learnings

1
Combined actual ROAS 2.75x vs 4.6x projected — 60% of projected revenue realised in ~3 months of ledger visibility. With the missing 28 Apr–24 May window, contact-mismatch losses, and an open tail, true attribution is higher; treat 2.75x as the audited floor.
2
Utsav exceeded plan on every money metric — 2.42x vs 2.1x, 116% of projected revenue, and the 1% conversion assumption verified at 1.00%. At ₹3.60L it was the cheaper, more predictable bet.
3
Organic out-earned paid ~2.3:1 — Organic Platforms + Referrals returned ₹44.75L with near-zero direct spend, while Meta+Google returned ₹20.96L on ₹19.45L (1.08x). Paid media's real job was feeding the top-of-funnel (59% of leads) — judge it on blended, not direct, ROAS.
4
Influencers + auto-branding: ₹3.00L spend, zero attributed conversions. The April report already showed weak funnel numbers (229 + 23 leads); the ledger confirms no revenue. Cut or restructure to performance-linked deals.
5
The ₹99 exam is a working intent filter — exam takers converted at 3.3% vs 0.8% for free registrants (4.2x), and carry 65% of scholarship revenue. Design future campaigns around micro-payment qualifiers.
6
Half of scholarship revenue is upsell — SSEI students ₹33.79L (51%) vs net-new ₹27.97L. That's a win (LTV expansion), but next campaign's 5x math should model net-new and upsell separately; net-new alone was 1.19x.
7
Ticket beat plan, volume missed it — ₹33,967 actual vs ₹30,000 assumed, while conversions ran 53% of the 415 combined target. The 4–6% lead→sale assumptions were the optimistic part; actuals: 2.1% scholarship, 1.00% Utsav.
8
IVR is a lead-scoring tool, not a conversion tool — paid-IVR pickups converted ~35% better than non-pickups, but the unpaid nudge script yielded 0.7% conversion. Keep engagement-check IVR, feed key-press data to counsellors, drop payment-nudge calling.
9
Utsav no-shows are money — 61% of Utsav revenue came from registrants who never attended. Event value lives in the registration list, so nurture streams must include no-shows by default.
10
The conversion window is ~90 days — collections peaked Jun–Jul with CFA L1 batch starts and thinned by August. Future scholarship cohorts: line up batch-start offers within 60 days of the exam, and re-run this attribution at day 90 as standard practice.

8. Methodology, data sources & caveats

How attribution was computed

  1. Payments ledger: 2,520 payments, 25 May – 19 Aug 2026, ₹7,18,46,989 total collections (3 rows had no amount and count as buyers but ₹0 revenue). Independently re-totalled with a second parser — exact match.
  2. Matching: buyer email OR any phone number (normalised to 10 digits, multi-number cells split) matched against (a) the 9,624-lead scholarship LeadSquared base with channel + SSEI classification — the same classified file behind the April report — and (b) the consolidated Utsav lead list (2,400 unique after removing 14 internal test rows).
  3. Buckets: matched both lists → "Scholarship + Utsav"; one list → that campaign; neither → "Individual" (excluded from campaign ROAS).
  4. Revenue rule: every collected rupee from a matched buyer counts (course fees, part-payments, extensions, books); unique persons = conversions; books/extension-only buyers flagged (9 of 219). Existing-SSEI purchases count as campaign upsell, reported separately.
  5. Cross-checks passed: bucket revenue sums to ledger total; channel and SSEI splits sum to 202; spot-checked matches verified by hand against raw LS export and Utsav list; funnel numbers tie to April report (9,624 / 6,472 / 4,008 / 2,869 mock).

Read the numbers with these caveats

1. This is a floor. The ledger begins 25 May — purchases in the first four weeks after the 26–27 Apr exam are not in the file. Contact-exact matching misses buyers who purchased with a different email/phone than they registered with.
2. Collections ≠ bookings. Revenue is money actually received in the window, including installments; some enrollments are partially collected.
3. Correlations are not causation. IVR/attendance/exam-stage lifts show who converts, not necessarily why.
4. Utsav universe: attribution ran on the consolidated 2,400-lead file supplied Aug 2026; April's headline counted 2,549 raw registrations (incl. 618 LP leads with broken UTM). Attendance in this file: 427 vs 448 in April (LP attendee records not all present).
5. Aug 2026 is partial (to 19 Aug) and the pipeline remains open — recommend re-running this exact attribution at 27 Oct 2026 (day-180).
