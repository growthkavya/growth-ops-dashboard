02 · Executive summary

Four conclusions that matter

TAKEAWAY 01

OOH's job has changed: from converter to familiariser

Approximately 90% of students interviewed feel hoardings no longer work as a standalone medium. They still notice them, but passively: in traffic, while waiting, on repeated commutes.

TAKEAWAY 02

Frequency may matter more than size

Around 70% said repeatedly seeing multiple smaller SSEI hoardings could subconsciously influence them. "I keep seeing this brand everywhere" builds familiarity and trust bias.

TAKEAWAY 03

Creative must work in a 3-5 second window

70-80% want vibrant or strong solid-colour designs with big text, strong contrast and one clear message. Information-heavy hoardings are ignored.

TAKEAWAY 04

Awareness comes from OOH; decisions come from proof

Near-unanimously, students trust credible reviews, peers and alumni over billboard claims. 85% feel online marketing creates stronger retention. OOH must feed the digital and social-proof journey, not replace it.

Online vs billboard: where recall really lives

Students spend far more time with Instagram, YouTube, search and social advertising than consciously observing billboards. Repeated digital touchpoints stack up; a single billboard location does not.

09 · Finding: location & commuter behaviour

Where and when students actually look

A recurring qualitative theme: context decides whether a creative is seen at all. The same design performs differently by placement, commute direction and surroundings.

METRO

More noticeable, if typography is large

Longer dwell time and captive attention reward big, readable type.

BUS STOPS

Lower active attention

Students reported paying less deliberate attention to bus-stop advertising.

TRAFFIC & SIGNALS

The prime OOH moment

Stuck and waiting is when hoardings get read. Slow corridors beat fast ones.

MORNING VS RETURN

Return commute wins

Morning commuters are rushed; students say they notice more on the way back.

ENVIRONMENT

Contrast with the surroundings

The creative must stand apart from its physical backdrop, or it disappears into it.

IMPLICATION

Adapt creative to context

Modify execution by dwell time × distance × traffic speed × environment. One design everywhere is a compromise everywhere.

10 · Opportunity: distinctive brand asset

The mascot opportunity

A smaller but strategically interesting group of students suggested SSEI develop a recognisable mascot or recurring brand character: something students identify before they even read "SSEI."

Students felt a mascot could improve recognition, create memorability, appeal to younger audiences, and over time become inseparable from the SSEI name. Position this as a long-term brand-building investment, not an immediate campaign requirement.

15 · Strategic recommendations

What SSEI should change

Each recommendation traces directly to a student finding. These are tests to run, not doctrines to adopt blindly.

01

Frequency over prestige

Test multiple repeated small and medium placements against one premium location, on equal budget. The 70% repeated-exposure finding says frequency builds the familiarity that one famous board cannot.

02

Vibrant vs solid minimalist

A/B the two creative styles students endorsed: bright colourful execution vs a strong solid-colour design carrying one powerful line.

03

Reduce information

Retire course-information-heavy hoardings. Use the billboard for memory, not explanation; the details live one search away.

04

Build distinctive brand assets

Explore a mascot or recurring character, a signature graphic, consistent typography, a recurring campaign device. Assets that compound with repetition.

05

Connect OOH to digital

Design every board to trigger the journey students actually take: recognition → search → content → reviews → enquiry.

06

Strengthen review visibility

Near-unanimously, reviews outweigh billboard claims. Invest in surfacing credible reviews, alumni voices and student stories wherever the searched-for brand lands.

07

Adapt creative to location

No single execution everywhere. Modify by dwell time × distance × traffic speed × environment: metro boards are not flyover boards.

17 · Final strategic takeaway

Students are not asking SSEI to put more information on a billboard. They are asking SSEI to become harder to ignore, and easier to remember.

OOH SHOULD CREATE MEMORY.

DIGITAL SHOULD CREATE ENGAGEMENT.

REVIEWS SHOULD CREATE TRUST.

The opportunity for SSEI is not simply to "put up more hoardings." It is to build a recognisable brand that students keep seeing, remembering and validating across multiple touchpoints, until choosing SSEI feels like recognising an old friend.
