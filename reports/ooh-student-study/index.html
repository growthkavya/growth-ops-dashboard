01

The verdict

We got the media buy right, the creative wrong, picked up free visibility we did not plan for, and moved no enquiries.

Worked

Locations

All three sites sat where our students move. Half of those who remembered the board could name the spot.

Did not work

Creative

Only ~30% recognised our board with the logo hidden. A competitor scored ~85% in the same test.

Bonus

LinkedIn

Strangers photographed the hoarding and argued about it for a month. We paid nothing for it.

No change

Enquiries

Leads and inbound calls stayed flat throughout. Worth stating plainly.

In one line

We bought good media and hung average creative on it. The sites put us in front of the right students, and the board failed to tell them who we were.

Right by J. D. Birla, catching students where they wait rather than where they rush past.

Park Street Metro Station
Signage · two faces · 167.75 sq ft · on a main commuter gate
Best of the three

Why

St. Xavier’s students use it daily, and Park Street is where the city goes out. One site covering weekday commute and weekend youth footfall.

Result

The one we got compliments on, and it is not close. Sitting on a main gate makes it genuinely unmissable. Our strongest creative goes here next time.

The pattern

Half the students who recognised the board could name where they saw it. When a hoarding sits on a daily route, the place becomes part of the memory. That beats raw traffic counts.

06

Where the creative lost us

Three problems, all fixable before the next booking.

One

The WhatsApp style board

Disliked by everyone who saw it. Chat bubbles work at arm’s length and turn into grey shapes on a road.

Two

Our name was too small

This is what the 30 against 85 gap means. People saw a board and could not connect it to SSEI.

Three

Height and sightline

The shelter panel sat above head height. Viewing angle needs to be on the checklist before we sign, not after.

The fix

One message per board, type readable across a road, colour that separates the board from its surroundings, and our name large enough that nobody hunts for it. That is a design brief, not a budget increase.

09

Our view

Everything above is what the data said. This is our reading of it, kept separate so you can disagree with the opinion without doubting the numbers.

01

Good media, average creative

Placement signals all came back positive. A competitor then scored 85% against our 30% on the same streets. Site selection is not the thing to fix.

02

The best return came from a channel we did not budget for

A month of unpaid LinkedIn argument, with professionals defending us. We had nothing ready to catch it. Next time we should be in that conversation on day one.

03

The line was clever but needed a second read

Half those comment threads were people explaining the joke to each other. That is a lot to ask of someone passing in traffic. Witty is fine, needing to be decoded is the risk.

04

The real problem is bigger than one campaign

55 to 60% of finance students in Kolkata had not heard of us even when prompted. Two months on three sites was never going to shift that. It tells us the size of the job.

05

Flat leads is a warning, not a verdict

Nobody buys a hoarding to close admissions. But we currently have no number to defend the next spend with, so until recall is measured every OOH budget conversation is one we lose.

10

What we do about it

What we already know enough to change, what needs testing, and the longer plays.

01

Make our name unmissable on every board

The direct fix for the 30 against 85 gap.

02

One message per board

Big type, few words, strong colour separation. Seven or eight in ten students told us this directly.

03

Stop reusing social artwork outdoors

Anything built for a feed gets redrawn for the road, or it does not go up.

04

Add sightline to the site checklist

Viewing height and angle, checked before signing.

05

Rebook Park Street and Exide, fix the shelter panel

Two sites earned their place outright, the third with a different panel.

06

Have the digital follow up ready before the next board goes up

Reviews, alumni and results matter more than the board, because that is where students told us they decide.

T1

Many boards against one statement site

Same money split two ways. 70% said repeat sightings build familiarity, but that is what they think they would do.

T2

A visible offer against a pure brand message

Test whether a discount cheapens us as much as it lifts attention.

T3

Sir’s name against the SSEI mark alone

If our mark is not carrying recall, sir may be the shortcut. Test rather than assume.

T4

Bold and witty against direct and clear

We know witty travels. We do not know whether it converts better.

T5

Run the card test before and after

The single most useful measurement change. Same masked card exercise a week before launch and again at the end.

T6

Instrument amplification from day one

Mentions, reposts, phrase searches, direct traffic, QR scans, so the next viral moment is measurable.

01

A house style for outdoor

Same colour, type and layout logic every time, so the tenth board benefits from the nine before it.

02

Decide how far we use sir as a brand asset

Our most recognisable asset and our most sensitive one. The CA campaign showed both sides in one week.

03

Design campaigns meant to travel

The LinkedIn reaction was an accident. The next one can be built for it.

04

Build the Kolkata awareness base properly

Being unknown to half our own city needs a year long plan, not a two month booking.

11

What we could not measure

All the caveats in one place, so nothing above needs hedging twice.

Gaps in the fieldwork
  • No control group, so we cannot separate the campaign from everything else we do.
  • No baseline before the boards went up, so no before and after exists.
  • No fake card shown, so recognition numbers are uncorrected for people agreeing to be polite.
  • We did not record how many correctly named SSEI after recognising the board. That is the number we most want next time.
  • Student against working professional split not recorded, nor how often each person passes the sites.
  • The CA campaign question was not asked of a counted group.
Operational checks not done
Nobody formally verified every panel was up, lit and unobstructed for the full contracted period with dated photographs. Cheap to do, contractually ours, and standard from the next booking.
What LinkedIn can and cannot say
Six screenshots supplied, all figures read off them. Real discussion, but not the whole conversation and not a sentiment survey. Nothing totalled into reach.
What Google Trends can and cannot say
Only related query lists were exported, not a weekly series, so no trend line can be drawn. Trends reports relative interest, not volume, and cannot establish that our boards caused any movement.
12

The bottom line

The sites were right, the artwork was not, and the most valuable thing that happened was something we never planned for.

That does not say stop doing outdoor. It says the next 6 lakh goes on the same kind of sites with a simpler, braver board carrying our name at a size nobody can miss, and the measurement set up before it goes live.

The question is not how many hoardings we can afford. It is whether a student who walks past one can tell somebody the next day whose board it was.
