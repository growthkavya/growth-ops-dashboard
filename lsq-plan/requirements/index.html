What this document is

One place for everything the LeadSquared team needs to audit, clean, rebuild and connect our account. Every decision in it has been taken by SSEI leadership. Where a line is still open it is marked, and the open items are collected on the last tab.

Who does what

  • LeadSquared team builds and configures, with SSEI support where needed.
  • SSEI sign-off: Kavya (Growth Ops). Managers Harman, Pritha and Shahzad sign off the counsellor-facing parts.
  • Nothing changes in the live account until the backup on the Data transfer tab is complete and SSEI has confirmed it.

Rules that apply to every tab

  • One writer per field. Every automatic field is read-only for people.
  • Nothing old is switched off until its replacement has passed the tests on the last tab, or unless it needs replacing.
  • Each course is an opportunity. No course information lives on the lead.
  • No field or rule is named after a person.
  • Consent is taken on every form and before any messaging.

Tools in the picture

  • WhatsApp: Gupshup
  • Calling and recording: Exotel
  • Webinars: Zoom
  • Payments: Razorpay, plus manual payments
  • LMS: Techhand, go-live expected mid-October 2026
  • Website: the new ssei.co.in

The loop that is missing today: what a student replies to a message, whether they attended, and what their result was all have to come back in through the same gate as events. Without that return leg the system sends but never learns, which is why we hold an exam attempt for only 38.5% of students and no results at all.

One gate in, one truth in the middle, everything else reads from it. The gateway is a small service, the same pattern as the Analyst Arena backend; it is the only thing allowed to create or update a person.

No longer a stage

Inactive

Not a stage. A student with no study activity for 30 days is Inactive while still Studying or preparing, which a stage cannot express. It is a saved list and a message trigger, read from Last Studied On, so it needs no field of its own.

The funnel is four steps. New, In Conversation, Counselling, Converted. Reports count those and nothing else, so stage-to-stage rates are readable.
Parked and Closed are the leakage. Parked is open pipeline with a date to come back; Closed is out, with a reason group and a detail.
Tried but not reached is a filter, not a stage. New with Call Attempts above zero. That was 44% of first calls last year and it never needed a bucket.
Bad data and Opted out are decided on the person. They close every open opportunity with that reason group, so Junk and Do Not Contact stop being course-level stages.
Ready to pay is not a stage either. It is a call outcome that keeps the opportunity in Counselling and creates a payment-link task; Payment Status shows Link sent.

How opportunities behave

One per person per course. A repeat enquiry about the same course updates the existing opportunity (Enquiries +1, priority lifted). It never creates a duplicate and never resets a converted one.
Formats are not courses. CFA Level 1 Fastrack and CFA Level 1 Live Focused are one opportunity. The product bought goes into Batch at payment.
Not decided yet is allowed once. If the course is unknown, one opportunity opens with Course set to Not decided yet, so the call still gets an owner and a deadline. The first call names the course. This is the only time Course changes.
Every new purchase is its own opportunity. A next level, retake or access extension opens a new opportunity; Buyer Type says which, and Product Type says what was bought.
An event opens an opportunity only when the event is about one course. A CFA webinar opens a CFA Level 1 opportunity. A general career webinar, a scholarship test or a college seminar opens one with Course set to Not decided yet, and the first call names the course. The event itself is always recorded as its own activity, whatever the course.
An ad for an event is still a paid lead. Source category comes from the link, not the landing page: a paid medium always wins, so a Meta ad for a webinar is Paid, Meta Ads, with the webinar campaign in First Campaign and a webinar activity on the profile. That is how we tell ad-driven event registrations from organic ones.
Mock and book buyers are marked as such. Product Type on the converted opportunity says Mock pack, Books or notes, Single topics or Full course. Someone whose only purchases are mocks or books is a buyer we already trust, and they get the full-course playbook instead of a new-lead one.
Combos convert every course in them. Under one Order ID, as above.
Bought a course they never asked about. The paid course opens at payment and converts. A Not decided yet opportunity closes with the reason group "Bought another SSEI course", which only the system can set. Named-course opportunities stay open; the counsellor closes them with the same reason if the student says so.
Every open opportunity has one open task. The call log form's next action creates it, and Priority sets the first one's deadline. Overdue tasks are how neglect shows up.
The owner stays after the sale. Managers reassign open opportunities only, so Owner on a converted opportunity always shows who made the sale.
Person-level decisions close everything. Bad data (a fake or wrong number, a duplicate) and Opted out are set once on the lead and close every open opportunity with that reason group.
Support calls never open an opportunity. Books, logins, refunds and doubts are logged as support activities and never move a sales stage.
Existing students never get sales messages for a course they are studying. A cross-sell is answered at once if they ask, and suggested only once they are 60% through the current course.
Past purchases arrive on day one. The student master load creates converted opportunities for every past purchase, so labels and upsell reports work from launch.

How the Fit and Intent scores are built

Decided 21 September: the scores are calculated by automation inside LeadSquared and stored on the opportunity, so a counsellor can see why a lead sits where it does and the model can be tuned from real numbers. The reference model has 101 factors; these five blocks are how it gets built.

BlockWhat it adds upInputs we already holdWhen it runs
Fit: who they areUp to 100, and it never decaysLife stage, highest qualification, age band, the course asked for, eligibility for that course, and whether they are a current student, an alum or new to SSEIOpportunity created, and whenever one of those changes

Build it in blocks, not as one giant rule. One automation per block, each adding to a running total, so changing one block cannot break the others.
Score only on what we actually hold. The reference model also scores weekly study hours, budget, who decides and a declared purchase timeline. None of those are collected in the new field set, so they are out until a form asks for them, and their points are spread across the blocks above.
Cap every block. No single answer can push a score past its block cap, so one loud signal cannot outrank a real fit.
Nobody edits a score. Fit Score, Intent Score, Playbook and Priority are read-only for every user. A counsellor who disagrees changes the answer underneath, not the number.

The UTM standard for every link we share

Kept in one place so it is never written twice: the SSEI UTM Dictionary, which the organic and performance teams follow. It holds the five fields, the 14 source values, the 12 medium values, the campaign codes and a link builder.

What the LeadSquared team takes from it. The medium table is the lookup for N2: each utm_medium value, with utm_source, gives the source category and channel.
Order of precedence. A paid medium always gives Paid. Otherwise an event, scholarship, seminar or partner form gives Events or B2B / Partners. Otherwise the medium decides. No UTMs gives Website, or Organic from a search or AI search referrer.
Values outside the dictionary. Stored as received in the UTM fields, mapped to the category Unknown channel, and listed in the nightly data health alert so marketing can correct the link.
