The lifecycle system

One person, one phone number, one system, from the first form they fill to the day they clear their last exam. It comes in two halves. Read the half you work in, and know where the other one starts.

Half one

Before they pay

Decides whether somebody should buy, and what to say to them. Scores Fit and Intent. Twelve lead playbooks, PB-01 to PB-12. Nine journey stages.

9,719 leads Their behaviour sets the clock

Half two

After they pay

Decides what they buy next, and whether they pass. Scores Headroom and Momentum. Twelve student playbooks, SP-01 to SP-12. Nine student stages.

85,802 students The exam calendar sets the clock

The handover. The moment a payment confirms, the lead record closes and the student record opens on the same phone number, last ten digits. Both halves feed one priority queue, so a new lead and an existing student can be ranked against each other instead of sitting on two lists nobody can compare.

The four things to hold on to

Fit is not Intent

Fit is whether the course suits them. Intent is whether they are ready to act. Someone can be a perfect fit and completely uninterested, or desperate to buy something they cannot take.

Playbook is not Journey

The playbook is our plan. The journey is where they actually are. Those are two different questions and they used to share one word.

Unknown is a real answer

When we do not know something, the system says so and asks one question. It never guesses in a message.

Every decision has a reason

Any cohort, score, warning or action can be traced back to the raw data that produced it, in plain English.

Understand

How it works

There are 24 life stages, 22 courses and 45 sources. Writing a separate plan for every combination is millions of plans, which is why nobody does it and why everyone ends up sending the same generic message. Instead we evaluate nine things independently, then assemble the answer.

The formula

Signals  →  9 dimensions  →  checks  →  Fit + Intent  →  cohort  →  playbook  →  action

Signals are raw facts: a form answer, a click, a reply, an event attendance. Dimensions are the nine things we reason about. Checks are the guardrails. Everything after that follows.

Two scores, never one

Fit · out of 100

Does this course suit this person. Built from life stage, eligibility, whether it is the right step, whether they have the time and money, and whether they already study with us.

It does not decay. Fit only changes when we learn something new about them.

Intent · out of 100

How close are they to acting. Built from where they came from, what they have done, what they have said about timing, and what they have asked us for.

It decays. After 7 quiet days it falls 2 points a day, and any reply resets it.

Why two numbers. A class 12 student asking for a CFA payment link scores 62 on intent and is capped at 25 on fit, because he cannot register. On one number he would sit at the top of the sales queue. On two he goes to Redirect and the counsellor slot stays free for someone who can actually buy. See Rohan Gupta.

What to do about them, in plain words

Call now

Ready to buy, or a payment has failed. Within 30 minutes during calling hours.

Call today

Asked about fees, batches or instalments. Within 4 working hours.

Call this week

Real signal, worth a conversation. Within 24 working hours.

Automated only

Not enough signal for counsellor time yet. The playbook keeps running.

Redirect

Keen, but cannot take this course. Name what is open to them instead.

Do not contact

Opted out, unreachable, already bought, or under a legal hold.

The order things happen in

Eighteen steps, always in this order. Each one opens to show the technical version. Steps 2 and 8 can stop the process entirely, which is intended.

1 Load what we have

Pull everything known about this person: the form they filled, the CRM record, any earlier leads, and whether they already bought from us.

In: Raw lead record, CRM history, student master Out: One combined record Defined in: Field registry

If it fails: The evaluation stops and the lead is flagged. Never evaluate on a partial load.

lead = load(lead_id)\nlead.history = crm.history(lead.mobile_last10)\nlead.master = student_master.match(lead.mobile_last10)
2 Check we are allowed to contact them

Before anything else, check consent, opt outs, bad numbers and whether they already bought.

In: consent_marketing, whatsapp_optin_status, opt_out_flag, existing_student_status Out: suppression_reason Defined in: Guardrails GR-001 to GR-005

If it fails: Nothing is sent. This is the correct outcome, not a failure.

if suppression_check(lead) is blocked:\n    log('suppressed', reason)\n    return NO_ACTION
3 Tidy the raw data

Standardise the phone number, lowercase the email, match the city to the city master, map the source to one of the 45 known values.

In: Raw fields Out: Normalised fields Defined in: Field registry, layer Normalised

If it fails: Unmapped values are logged. A rising unmapped rate means the taxonomy has drifted.

lead.mobile_last10 = normalise_phone(lead.mobile_e164)\nlead.city_tier, lead.centre, lead.tz = city_master(lead.city)\nlead.source_detail = source_map(lead.raw_source)
4 Spot disagreements

Where two sources say different things about the same fact, mark the field as disputed rather than picking one.

In: All input fields Out: field_conflict_flags Defined in: Guardrails GR-013 to GR-015

If it fails: The disputed field is not used in any message and a verification task is raised.

for f in DECISION_CRITICAL:\n    if sources_disagree(lead, f):\n        lead.field_conflict_flags.add(f)
5 Resolve what can be resolved

Use the precedence order: what they said themselves beats what a counsellor recorded, which beats a form, which beats a guess.

In: Disputed fields Out: Resolved values, remaining flags Defined in: Methodology, precedence order

If it fails: Anything still tied stays flagged and stays out of the message.

for f in lead.field_conflict_flags:\n    lead[f] = highest_precedence(lead.sources[f])\n    if still_tied: keep_flagged(f)
6 Work out the nine dimensions

Turn the tidy fields into the nine things we actually reason about: life stage, course interest, eligibility, capacity, relationship, source, engagement, urgency, location.

In: Normalised fields Out: Nine dimension values, each with a confidence Defined in: Dimensions

If it fails: A dimension that cannot be computed is set to Unknown, never guessed.

for d in DIMENSIONS:\n    lead.dim[d] = evaluate(d, lead)\n    lead.dim[d].confidence = confidence_of(d, lead)
7 Apply the dependencies between them

Some dimensions depend on others. Eligibility needs the qualification. Suitability needs the life stage and the course.

In: Dimension values Out: Updated dimension values Defined in: Dimensions, depends_on column

If it fails: Unknown inputs produce Unknown outputs, which block payment links rather than guessing.

lead.dim.eligibility = check_eligibility(lead.dim.course, lead.dim.life_stage, lead.qualification)\nlead.dim.suitability = check_suitability(lead.dim.course, lead.dim.life_stage)
8 Run the checks

Run every active guardrail. Blocking ones stop the process. Conflicts and warnings are recorded and shown.

In: Dimension values, all fields Out: List of fired rules, blocks Defined in: Guardrails

If it fails: The user is told which rule fired and why, in plain English. Nothing is ever silently rejected.

fired = [g for g in GUARDRAILS if g.active and g.condition(lead)]\nlead.guardrails_triggered = fired\nif any(g.severity == 'Blocking' for g in fired):\n    return blocked_action(fired)
9 Score the Fit

How well does this course suit this person. Life stage, eligibility, suitability, capacity and relationship. This score never decays with time.

In: Dimensions 1, 3, 4, 5 Out: fit_score, fit_confidence Defined in: Scoring model

If it fails: Unknown inputs score neutral, never zero. A missing answer is not a bad answer.

lead.fit_score = sum(component(c, lead) for c in FIT_COMPONENTS)\nlead.fit_confidence = confidence_from_completeness(lead)
10 Score the Intent

How close are they to acting. Source, engagement, stated urgency and what they have asked for. This score decays after 7 days of silence.

In: Dimensions 6, 7, 8 Out: intent_score, intent_confidence Defined in: Scoring model

If it fails: A lead with no engagement data starts at their source baseline, not at zero.

lead.intent_score = sum(component(c, lead) for c in INTENT_COMPONENTS)\nlead.intent_score -= decay(lead.days_since_last_engagement)\nlead.intent_confidence = confidence_from_evidence(lead)
11 Turn the two scores into a priority

Fit and intent together decide what a human should do today.

In: fit_score, intent_score, eligibility, suppression Out: priority Defined in: Methodology, Fit and Intent matrix

If it fails: Ineligible always becomes Redirect regardless of how keen they are.

lead.priority = priority_matrix(lead.fit_score, lead.intent_score,\n                                lead.eligibility, lead.suppression_reason)
12 Find the cohorts that fit

Test the lead against every cohort definition and keep the ones that match.

In: Dimension values Out: Candidate cohort list Defined in: Cohorts

If it fails: If nothing matches, discovery is used and the miss is logged for review.

candidates = [c for c in COHORTS if c.matches(lead.dim)]
13 Throw out the combinations that make no sense

Remove any candidate that a guardrail says is contradictory, impossible or commercially pointless.

In: Candidate list, fired guardrails Out: Valid candidate list Defined in: Guardrails

If it fails: If everything is removed, discovery is used and the reason is recorded.

candidates = [c for c in candidates if not violates_guardrail(c, lead)]
14 Rank what is left and pick one

The most specific match wins. A tie is logged, because a tie usually means a rule is missing.

In: Valid candidates Out: primary_cohort, other_eligible_cohorts Defined in: Cohorts

If it fails: Ties are broken by the lower rule ID and reported weekly.

candidates.sort(key=specificity, reverse=True)\nlead.primary_cohort = candidates[0]\nlead.other_eligible_cohorts = candidates[1:3]\nif tie: log_tie()
15 Pick the plan

The cohort selects the playbook, which is the actual sequence of messages, resources and calls.

In: primary_cohort Out: assigned_playbook Defined in: Playbooks

If it fails: Discovery is the fallback playbook.

lead.assigned_playbook = PLAYBOOKS[lead.primary_cohort]
16 Work out where they are

Set the journey stage from what has actually happened, not from what anyone hopes.

In: Events Out: journey_stage, journey_stage_since Defined in: Journey stages

If it fails: Everyone starts at New. A stage is never set by opinion.

lead.journey_stage = stage_from_events(lead.events)
17 Decide the single next thing to do

Combine the cohort, the stage and the priority into one clear instruction and a date.

In: Cohort, stage, priority Out: next_action, next_action_at Defined in: Playbooks

If it fails: If no action can be determined the lead is parked with a reason, never left blank.

lead.next_action, lead.next_action_at = next_action(\n    lead.primary_cohort, lead.journey_stage, lead.priority)
18 Write down why

Record in plain English which rules fired and what produced the cohort, the scores and the action.

In: Everything above Out: evaluation_explanation, audit trace Defined in: Methodology

If it fails: An evaluation without an explanation is treated as a bug, not a result.

lead.evaluation_explanation = explain(rules_fired, scores, cohort)\naudit.write(lead, trace_id)

When two things disagree

Precedence: what they said themselves → what a counsellor recorded → what a form captured → what a campaign implied → what behaviour suggests → a default.

If two sources of equal weight disagree, the field is flagged, every message that would mention it falls back to neutral wording, and a person is asked to resolve it. The system says less rather than guessing better. See Vikram Iyer for what that looks like in practice.

The quadrant is a view. The priority chip is the instruction. A quadrant is only the two scores. Priority applies the hard rules on top, so an ineligible lead shows Redirect even when the scores look strong, and a lead with a disputed field shows a lower priority until a person resolves it.

Fit scan

How suitable each person is for the course in question, and the reasons behind the number. Fit never changes with time, only with new information.

Intent scan

How close each person is to acting, the strongest signals, and how recent they are. Intent falls after seven quiet days.

Journey

Where a person is, in nine stages. This is the shared language for the question everyone asks: where is this lead. The CRM holds 26 technical states underneath, and each stage lists which ones it contains.

How a lead moves

New -> Contacted -> Engaged -> Interested -> In conversation -> Ready to buy -> Paying -> Enrolled | | | | | | | +----------+-------------+-------------+----------------+--------------------+---------------+---> Parked (with a reason, and usually a date to return)

Forward moves happen on evidence, never on opinion. Any stage can move to Parked. Parked is not lost: it carries a reason and often a date to come back.

Playbooks

One page per cohort, nine questions each. A playbook is our plan for a group of people: what to offer, what to say, what never to say, and what happens next. The rule level detail sits behind the expander at the bottom of each card.

Dimensions

The nine things the system reasons about. Every dimension states exactly what it is allowed to change and what it can never change. That boundary is the whole point: a dimension allowed to change everything produces chaos, and one allowed to change nothing produces generic messaging.

The boundary that matters most. Location can change wording, logistics and which testimonial we use. It can never change a score, a priority or the tone we take with someone. Where a person lives changes what is practical, not how they are treated.

Adding a new check

A check is a row, not a code change. Fill in these nine things and it runs on the next evaluation.

rule_id · rule_name · severity · category · plain_description · dimensions_affected · fields_affected · condition · action · user_facing_explanation · owner · active

The one that matters most is user_facing_explanation. If a check cannot explain itself to a counsellor in one sentence, it is not ready to go live.

How to change this system

To change a message

Edit SSEI_Message_Library.csv, then regenerate. A copy change needs template re approval before it can send.

To add a check

Add a row to SSEI_Guardrails.csv. No code change. It runs on the next evaluation.

To add a field

Add a row to SSEI_Field_Registry.csv with its layer, meaning and what it can change. A field with no stated purpose does not get created.

To change a playbook

Edit SSEI_Playbooks.csv. Keep it to the nine questions. If an answer needs a paragraph, it belongs in the advanced expander.

To change scoring

Edit SSEI_Lead_Scoring_Model.csv. Change one factor at a time and run the test cases.

To rebuild this site

python3 src/data-crm/build_cohortisation_dashboard.py, then upload the HTML. It is generated, never hand edited.

The student half

Everything that happens once somebody has given us money. 85,802 students in the master database, nine times the size of the lead pool, and it costs nothing to reach them.

One system, two halves. The lead half decides whether somebody should buy. This half decides what they buy next, and whether they pass. The moment a payment confirms, the lead record closes and the student record opens on the same phone number, last ten digits, the same key that builds the master. Nothing is re-keyed and nothing is lost.

The one idea

The exam calendar runs the business

This is the only concept the team has to hold on to, and it is the whole difference between the two halves.

Before somebody pays

Their behaviour sets the clock. We wait for a signal: a form, a reply, a question about the fee. Nothing happens on a schedule, because we do not control when a stranger becomes interested.

After somebody pays

The exam calendar sets the clock. Their exam day was published a year ago by CFA Institute, GARP or ICAI. Their result day is fixed. Their access expiry was fixed the moment they paid. Nobody has to wait for a signal, because every date is already known.

So the post-conversion system is a diary, not a listening exercise. Once we know which sitting a student is aiming at, we know every date that matters to them for the next two years, and the work goes in the calendar before it happens. That is why attempt is the field that blocks everything else.

Headroom and Momentum

The lead half scores Fit and Intent. This half scores the same two shapes with different names, so the team learns the idea once.

Headroom

What this student could still buy from us. Built from where they sit on the ladder, what they already own, and what they have spent. It does not decay. A student who cleared Level 1 and owns nothing else has high headroom whether they passed last month or three years ago.

Headroom decides what we pitch. Never how urgently.

How it is built, out of 100
Ladder headroomTwo levels left 50, one level left 30 to 35, ladder finished 550
Spend bandProven willingness to pay. Over Rs 50,000 scores full marks25
Adjacent products unownedAnalyst Stack, Stock Market, Excel and Python25

Every input is already in the master. Headroom can be computed this week.

Momentum

Are they studying enough to pass. Built from watch time, mocks attempted, doubts asked and replies. It decays fast. A student who has not opened the course in three weeks is a different student from one who stopped yesterday.

Momentum decides whether we intervene. Never what we sell.

The holistic part

One queue for the whole business

A new lead and an existing student compete for the same counsellor hour. Today they sit on different lists owned by different people, so nobody can answer the only question that matters: what is the best use of the next hour.

Question one: does something expire?

Anything with a date we cannot move. Result day. Exam day. Validity expiry. A batch closing. A lead going cold.

Question two: how much is on the table?

The value of the money moment behind it.

Dates beat money. A Rs 1,000 extension that expires on Friday outranks a Rs 37,000 Level 2 pitch with no deadline, because the extension disappears on Friday and the Level 2 pitch is still there next month. When two items share a deadline, the bigger number goes first.

The twelve student playbooks

What we actually do. SP-01 to SP-12. Filter by stage or owner, or search.

Two rules across all twelve. Nothing is sold in SP-01, SP-02, SP-06 or SP-07. The first week after payment and the days around a result are for trust, not for a pitch. Selling in those windows is what makes students stop reading our messages, which breaks every playbook after it. And SP-07 is the hinge: it is the only playbook whose job is to collect a fact rather than move a student. Everything worth the most money, SP-08, SP-09 and SP-10, is downstream of one question asked on one day.

Build sequence

Four steps. Each produces something the team can work before the next begins. Steps one to three need no engineering. Step four does.

  1. Turn on what needs no new dataHeadroom, ladder position, lifetime spend and months since last purchase are all computable from the master today. That produces the ladder gap lists and the win-back list on its own.
    Needs: Nothing. Can start now. Unlocks: SP-12 and the ladder half of SP-08
  2. Capture attempt and load the calendarRequired field at checkout: course, month, year. Plus the exam calendar loaded once a year from CFA Institute, GARP and ICAI.
    Needs: A one line checkout change. Unlocks: Stages S3 to S8, and SP-03, SP-05, SP-06, SP-07, SP-10
  3. Add validity expiry and run SP-11Purchase date plus the validity term of the product. Arithmetic on data we already hold. Independent of steps one and two.
    Needs: The validity term for every product. Unlocks: SP-11, and a Rs 1.0 crore reactive line becomes a scheduled one
  4. Connect the learning platformWatch time and last active date for paid courses. The only step that needs a technical integration.
    Needs: Platform export by phone or email. Unlocks: Momentum, and with it SP-02, SP-03 and SP-04
